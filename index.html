Naz Deniz Atik

pronounced [[naz deˈniz a'tic]]('https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Turkish')

Hello, my name is Deniz. I am a cognitive scientist and psycholinguist.

As a person, I am forever fascinated by just how much variability languages exhibit, both across and within themselves. So, I spend a lot of time thinking about how we, as humans, navigate and sometimes even take advantage of this variability to communicate, learn, and reason in different linguistic and social contexts throughout development.

As a researcher, I combine developmental, psycholinguistic, sociolinguistic, and cognitive science approaches to explore these questions. I am particularly interested in how young children process and learn from unfamiliar-accented speech (This is what I prefer to call it. We all have accents, so all speech is “accented”!). My dissertation and publications have primarily focused on two questions:

  • Can children predict an upcoming word in an unfamiliar accent based on the preceding words? For instance, can they predict that cake is likely to follow eat? This is an important skill we call semantic prediction. The answer is: Yes, 4-year-olds can!

  • Can children learn new words from unfamiliar-accented speakers by using the surrounding sentential context? For instance, can they figure out that dax refers to food from hearing “Eat the dax”? This is another important skill in language acquisition called semantic inference. So far, the answer is: Yes, 4-year-olds can, and sometimes even 2-year-olds can!

Other times, I think about what it means for speakers of a language when their language has a particular feature that doesn’t exist (or isn’t as prevalent) in other languages. Since my native Turkish is packed with suffixes, the language-specific features I think about often involve those little guys. Check out this poster I presented at MPaL in 2023 to see how the Turkish suffix -dir influences Turkish speakers’ thinking about genericity.

Where am I doing all this? I received my PhD in Cognitive Psychology from Northwestern University, under the supervision of Dr. Sandra Waxman. I am currently working with Dr. Sandy LaTourrette as a Visiting Scholar at the University of Southern California.
